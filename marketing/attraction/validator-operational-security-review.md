## How resilient are your validator operations when something goes wrong?

**Validator security is more than uptime.** A recovery that accidentally enables duplicate signing, an overlooked privileged account, or a poorly coordinated client update can create losses that ordinary infrastructure checks will miss.

Lionscraft helps Ethereum staking operators and institutions examine operational security, identify gaps and prioritize practical improvements—connecting validator-specific risks with the people, procedures, suppliers and systems that actually run the service.

*This is a scoped readiness and implementation-support offering—not an independent ValOS verification, certification or guarantee against slashing.*

### When this is useful

- You need to demonstrate operational controls to an allocator, custodian, protocol, insurer, institutional customer or internal risk committee.
- Your team has expanded beyond informal operating knowledge, and control evidence is hard to gather.
- You have changed signing or failover architecture, custody, infrastructure providers, operating personnel, or recovery procedures.
- You want to identify gaps before an independent standards-based assessment.
- An incident, near-miss, unexpected downtime or handover has raised questions about resilience.

### What we can review

**Signing safety:** Validator activation, signing authority, anti-slashing safeguards, failover, change controls and recovery.

**Keys and access:** Key custody and lifecycle, privileged access, separation of duties, emergency access and staff offboarding.

**Infrastructure and operations:** Client software, monitoring, incident response, backup and recovery, supplier dependencies and operational handovers.

**Evidence and governance:** Risk ownership, documented controls, responsibilities, training and review readiness.

The scope depends on your operating model. Where useful, the review can refer to the publicly available ValOS control structure.

### The engagement

1. **20-minute fit conversation:** Discuss your validator operating model, the actual concern, desired decision, timeline and whether an independent reviewer is needed.
2. **Agreed diagnostic scope:** Define systems, evidence, stakeholders, delivery plan and fees before work begins. Do not share sensitive configurations or secrets via general contact forms.
3. **Prioritized findings:** Subject to scope, a concise gap register, evidence/readiness view and practical next steps, distinguishing urgent signing-safety concerns from longer-term governance work.
4. **Optional improvement support:** Assistance with designing, implementing or documenting controls. Any formal independent verification remains separate.

### Free self-check: ten questions for your team

This is an educational prompt, not an official ValOS assessment or scorecard.

1. Can you show that a failover or restore cannot accidentally activate conflicting signers?
2. Can you enumerate people and systems with signing-key or signing-infrastructure access?
3. Have emergency recovery and signer-activation responsibilities been documented and rehearsed?
4. Do software and configuration changes follow a review and approval process?
5. Do you know which suppliers or dependencies create correlated risk?
6. Does monitoring cover signing and operational hazards, not only uptime?
7. Are incident escalation and decision-making clear across shifts and personnel changes?
8. Is privileged access reviewed and promptly removed on role changes?
9. Are incidents and near misses translated into control improvements?
10. Could an external reviewer evaluate your operating controls from reliable evidence?

A 'No' or 'Partly' is a useful place to investigate. Prioritize signing safety and control weaknesses protecting assets.
