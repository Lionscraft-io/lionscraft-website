The story in brief

Situation

stc Bahrain sought to expand its telecommunications infrastructure business into blockchain and Web3 services. Establishing this new business required a commercial strategy, protocol partnerships, technical infrastructure, operating capabilities and new organisational expertise.

Role

Lionscraft led the design and delivery of stc Bahrain's Web3 Launchpad programme, working across business strategy, commercial negotiations, technical architecture, implementation, operations, ecosystem development and education.

Work

A multi-protocol blockchain infrastructure programme; validator and node operations across several networks; Intersect, a dedicated Avalanche-based blockchain; the Intersect Makeathon + Hackathon; Ramadan Feast, an on-chain consumer application; and tailored enterprise and community training.

Outcome

Web3 Launchpad grew into a multi-protocol infrastructure programme, with stc Bahrain reporting more than 20 nodes and related infrastructure deployments in 2025. It produced operational blockchain services, a dedicated network, applications, developer initiatives and education programmes. The Launchpad received three industry awards during 2024–2025.

The challenge

Establishing a new blockchain infrastructure business inside a telecommunications company

stc Bahrain had an established telecommunications and enterprise infrastructure business, with capabilities in connectivity, cloud services, data centres and technical operations. Blockchain infrastructure offered an opportunity to extend those capabilities into services for public blockchain networks and digital-asset ecosystems.

However, entering this market involved more than adapting existing infrastructure.

Blockchain networks have different technical architectures, validation mechanisms, commercial arrangements and operating requirements. Node operators may earn revenue through protocol grants, staking rewards, delegation commissions or contracted infrastructure services. Each model creates different obligations around capital, custody, availability, risk and ongoing costs.

stc Bahrain needed to determine which networks to support, how to structure commercial relationships, what infrastructure to provide and how to manage the resulting services within an enterprise organisation.

Lionscraft was engaged to develop and execute this programme, combining blockchain expertise with the commercial, technical and operational disciplines required to establish a new infrastructure business.

The story

01

Establishing Web3 Launchpad

The engagement began in 2023 with the development of stc Bahrain's blockchain infrastructure strategy.

Lionscraft worked with stc Bahrain on the business architecture for its new Web3 operations, including the services it could offer, potential protocol customers, commercial models, operating requirements and routes to market.

The resulting programme, Web3 Launchpad, was organised around two partnership pathways:

Pearling Path focused on emerging blockchain protocols seeking infrastructure support, validator services and access to regional partners and markets.

Qala'a Path focused on strategic relationships with more established blockchain ecosystems, including Avalanche.

These pathways provided a framework for evaluating opportunities and structuring engagements according to each protocol's maturity, technical requirements and commercial potential.

Lionscraft's work covered protocol identification, business-case development, commercial proposals, partnership negotiations and the practical requirements for delivering infrastructure services.

That included evaluating staking and delegation arrangements, grant and incentive structures, infrastructure expenditure, ongoing operating costs and revenue models. The team also worked through enterprise requirements involving procurement, legal approvals, wallet management, digital-asset custody, treasury processes and operating responsibilities.

In early 2024, stc Bahrain publicly announced its strategic partnership with Lionscraft. The agreement was celebrated through a metaverse signing ceremony, including an NFT representation of the partnership.

Web3 Launchpad subsequently entered its first phase of infrastructure delivery.

02

Building and operating multi-protocol infrastructure

Lionscraft led protocol business development and coordinated the implementation of blockchain infrastructure services across multiple networks.

The first major partnerships included Core, Aleph Zero and Avalanche in April 2024. The programme subsequently expanded into relationships and technical work involving ZetaChain, Allora, Conflux, NEAR, Nillion and other blockchain ecosystems.

Each engagement required a different combination of commercial negotiation, infrastructure design, deployment, funding, operations and reporting.

For Core, the team progressed from partnership arrangements into the technical requirements of a functioning mainnet validator. This included wallet and funding preparation, test transactions, infrastructure provisioning, validator registration and operational monitoring. Project records show the Core mainnet validator operating by April 2024.

Lionscraft's delivery work extended into the operating environment surrounding these services. The programme involved coordinating specialist engineering resources, establishing monitoring, managing network changes, supporting incident response and tracking the operational and financial performance of different protocols.

The technical portfolio also diversified beyond conventional proof-of-stake validators.

For example, stc Bahrain's June 2025 Nillion announcement documented the launch of infrastructure for PetNet, a privacy-preserving computation network.

Across 2024 and 2025, Lionscraft managed the progression of protocol opportunities into a growing infrastructure programme. Internal operating records tracked production and testnet nodes, supporting services, network infrastructure, costs and operational responsibilities.

By 2025, stc Bahrain's published programme materials reported more than 20 nodes, spanning different infrastructure roles and networks.

Lionscraft's responsibilities included not only introducing new protocols and coordinating deployments, but also supporting the ongoing operation of the portfolio. This covered monitoring, troubleshooting, protocol upgrades, reward and cost tracking, commercial reviews and decisions about service continuation or transition.

03

Launching Intersect, a dedicated blockchain network

Web3 Launchpad expanded beyond operating infrastructure for third-party protocols with the development of Intersect, a dedicated Avalanche-based blockchain network.

Lionscraft worked on the commercial concept, network architecture, partner coordination and delivery programme for Intersect, which launched publicly in August 2024.

The network was developed within the Avalanche ecosystem, with AvaCloud providing managed blockchain infrastructure for its initial deployment.

Intersect included its own native token, PEARL, along with network services supporting transactions, smart contracts and application development.

The project required coordinating several connected elements: blockchain infrastructure, operating responsibilities, token and network requirements, developer access, ecosystem partnerships and an application roadmap.

Lionscraft worked across these activities, bringing together stc Bahrain, Avalanche ecosystem partners and specialist technical providers to deliver the network and associated programmes.

The network's subsequent evolution also included work toward greater operational control and changes to its infrastructure arrangements.

AvaCloud published a case study documenting Intersect, including its infrastructure deployment, the involvement of Lionscraft and applications developed around the network.

04

Delivering the Intersect Makeathon + Hackathon

Following Intersect's launch, Lionscraft helped design and deliver a developer and creator programme to encourage projects on the new network.

The Intersect Makeathon + Hackathon ran during August–September 2024, bringing together developers, designers, creators, technology partners, sponsors and judges.

Rather than limiting participation to programmers, the event combined conventional hackathon tracks with a makeathon for non-coding participants.

Competition areas included blockchain applications, gaming, interoperability, digital identity, media, security and product design. Lionscraft contributed to the programme structure, competition activities, sponsor and partner coordination, technical learning resources and event execution.

One of the bonus challenges involved the design of a Lionscraft-branded hardware wallet.

The event also included workshops and seminars to help participants understand the available technologies and develop their submissions.

Through this programme, Lionscraft delivered the developer-engagement and ecosystem activities associated with Intersect's initial launch.

05

Developing Ramadan Feast, an on-chain consumer application

In early 2025, the programme introduced Ramadan Feast, a blockchain-based game developed for the Intersect network.

The application used a food-collection concept inspired by Ramadan. Participants could collect digital ingredients, exchange items and compete through a leaderboard.

Lionscraft's application delivery work covered the technical components required to support these interactions, including smart contracts, NFT metadata, wallet integration, event processing and leaderboard functionality.

The implementation included Solidity-based ERC-721 contracts for digital collectibles, associated image and metadata assets, and application services connecting blockchain events with a database and user-facing leaderboard.

The first round generated substantial application activity. A bounded project-data snapshot recorded approximately 272,600 NFT event records associated with 10,293 distinct wallet addresses. These figures describe the recorded events and addresses, rather than independently verified transactions or unique human users.

The campaign also produced operational data for analysing participant behaviour, including unusually concentrated or potentially automated activity. This informed further work on gameplay, participation rules and eligibility mechanisms.

Ramadan Feast provided a deployed consumer application alongside Intersect's infrastructure and developer ecosystem work.

The application was also featured in AvaCloud's published account of the stc Bahrain programme.

06

Developing enterprise and community capabilities

Alongside the infrastructure and application work, Lionscraft developed training programmes to support stc Bahrain's new business and expand Web3 knowledge across different audiences.

The curriculum ranged from blockchain fundamentals and commercial strategy to validator operations, custody, staking economics, compliance and digital-asset processes.

In May 2024, Lionscraft delivered an executive business strategy workshop covering Web3 Launchpad's commercial model, infrastructure-service opportunities, enterprise architecture and financial considerations.

Additional technical learning activities were incorporated into the Intersect developer programme.

In November 2025, Lionscraft delivered four tailored workshops with stc Bahrain:

The workshops combined technical instruction with scenarios relevant to the participants' responsibilities.

For finance and treasury teams, for example, the material addressed wallet approval procedures, digital-asset movements, liquidity, staking income and related controls – all subjects directly connected to operating blockchain infrastructure within an enterprise.

stc Bahrain and Lionscraft announced the completed training programme in December 2025.

07

Managing the operating lifecycle

Lionscraft's engagement continued beyond initial launches into the management and evolution of the infrastructure portfolio.

As the programme expanded, different networks required different approaches to monitoring, upgrades, technical support, commercial review and service management.

Operating decisions also had to account for changes in delegation, protocol incentives, infrastructure expenditure and network utilisation.

The delivery programme included commercial renegotiations, operational reviews, service rationalisation and transition planning as individual protocol requirements and business conditions changed.

This work formed part of Lionscraft's broader responsibility for implementing and operating Web3 Launchpad across its business and technical lifecycle.
